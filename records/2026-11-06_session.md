## Analysis: IMU z accel vs GPS in the climb phase (bag 092757, 136-180 s after first IMU)
- Static (100-130 s): IMU z mean -9.764 (z points down), |a| 9.800.
- Steady climb (vertical velocity > 1 m/s, 17 two-second windows): mean IMU z -9.769 (std over windows 0.34), mean vertical velocity 1.8 m/s, mean |a| 10.27 (inflated by noise: norm of a noisy vector).
- Correlation of mean IMU z with GPS vertical velocity: r = 0.07 (/fc/climb: 0.09) -> none, as expected: constant velocity gives no specific-force change.
- Correlation with GPS vertical ACCELERATION (d(v)/dt, 2 s windows, n=21): r = -0.57 (4 s windows n=10: -0.42); negative sign expected since IMU z points down. Signal is small: GPS accel std 0.17 m/s2 vs IMU z window-to-window std 0.34.
- Climb is nearly constant-velocity (about 2.5 m/s), so the accelerometer carries almost no scale information in the climb; this is consistent with the climb scale error being vision-limited.

## Gravity magnitude test (climb phase only), cam1 tf40 acc0.09, bag 092757 — N=1 each
Hypothesis: IMX-5 reads |a| = 9.800 at rest but config gravity_mag = 9.7801, so setting 9.80 might cure the climb overshoot. RESULT: it made the climb WORSE.
| VIO/GPS height ratio at s after init | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 45 |
| gravity_mag 9.7801 (b0903_cam1_tf40_acc3x_r1) | 1.07 | 1.01 | 1.08 | 1.10 | 1.10 |
| gravity_mag 9.8000 (b0903_cam1_acc3x_g98_r1)    | 1.46 | 1.62 | 1.67 | 1.72 | 1.71 |
So the gravity-mismatch explanation (9.80 -> better) is refuted in that direction, but the climb height is very sensitive to the gravity offset: +0.02 m/s2 changes VIO height at 40 s by ~38 m
(1/2*a*t^2 with a=0.02, t=40 s gives 16 m; observed is 2x that, same order). Supports the picture: climb scale is set by tiny vertical acceleration offsets (a few cm/s2) integrated over the climb, and vision is too weak in vertical motion to correct them.
Untested follow-up: gravity_mag below 9.7801 (e.g. 9.76) and a repeat of 9.7801 to rule out run-to-run noise.
