## SETTLED

**`max_slam: 0` is worse, decisively.** Open item from 2026-08-04, closed.
3 reps each, unrecorded, alternating:

    D_best  max_slam 50   scale 0.917 0.916 0.914   rms 8.3-12.1   alt 121.8-125.2
    E       max_slam  0   scale 0.684 0.684 0.674   rms 37.7       alt 111.2-112.0

Yesterday's "E wins horizontally" was an artefact of point-cloud recording.
KEEP `max_slam: 50`. Also measured: SLAM carries ~6x the load of MSCKF
(38.5 vs 6.5 features/update), so `max_slam: 0` removes ~85% of all visual
measurements — that is why it collapses, not a marginal loss.

**The barometer chi2 gate self-locks.** `P_zz` collapses to ~0, the admissible
residual becomes ~2.2 m, and 41% of measurements are rejected — 100% of the
rejects exceed 2.2 m. Error is established during the CLIMB (res -10.5 m at
t=37 s, -14.7 m at t=54 s) and after that the barometer can never correct it.
Not a gate-tuning bug: a 1-DoF sensor is being asked to fix a 3-DoF systematic
scale error, and chi2 is correctly reporting the inconsistency.

**Forcing the gate open (`baro_chi2_multipler: 500`, config `F_barounlock`)
fixes altitude outright** — +0.5 to +1.0% vs truth in all 5 reps, from +13-16%.
Horizontal improves in 4 of 5 (0.949-0.959 vs D_best 0.914-0.917) with a ~20%
failure mode at 0.714. `mult: 500` is a PROBE, not a setting — it would swallow
a real barometer blunder in flight.

**GPS validates the barometer.** Four altitude sources on a common clock:

    IMX-5 baro (in bag)  peak 107.69   cruise 101.67
    ArduPilot baro       peak 104.10   cruise  99.12
    ArduPilot EKF        peak 100.72   cruise  99.33
    GPS (18-19 sats)     peak 109.35   cruise 105.41   corr 0.9985 vs baro

Truth is sound to ~+-4 m. VIO at 122-125 m sits above every source.

**`vs_gps.py` under-reports scale by ~2.7%.** It spreads poses evenly across the
GPS span, but the filter starts 3.37 s late and its updates are not uniform
(2.45 s rms off linear). Correcting the span alone recovers the yaw exactly
(168.9 -> 173.3 deg, matching timestamp-paired alignment) and ~1/3 of the scale
bias. All same-tool comparisons remain valid; absolute error is overstated.
Proper fix needs a bag timestamp on the `p_IinG` log line.

## DISPROVEN — do not re-run these

- **OpenCV threading.** `num_opencv_threads: 1` still gives 3 distinct
  trajectory hashes over 3 reps. Not the source of non-determinism.
- **`up_msckf_sigma_px`.** Looked decisive (px1: 11/12 collapsed, px2: 0/20)
  but every px2 run also had the barometer on. `gold_px2` (px2, no baro)
  collapsed on the first try. Confounded, not causal.
- **CPU starvation, from RViz or from `gps_truth_rviz.py`.** Collapsed runs show
  0.7 ms median lag and process FASTER than healthy ones (47 vs 34 Hz) — high
  throughput is a symptom of having no features to work on, not a cause.
- **Frame loss.** The `track_frequency` gate keys off BAG timestamps, so good
  and bad runs process the identical 3033 of 6068 frames.
- **Memory.** `available` was 5.0 GB throughout. High `buff/cache` is just the
  6.2 GB bag streaming through page cache; zram swap is ~3.3x compressed.
- **The binary.** `openvins_ws` looked bad (2/13) but that was confounded with
  gold-family configs; `openvins_baro_ws` also collapsed once isolated.

almost every run used a gold-family config, and those collapse. A config effect
was misread as a machine effect, which sent the session chasing binaries,
OpenCV threads, memory and a reboot. No reboot was needed.

**The gold standard needs BOTH `sigma_px: 2` AND the barometer — neither alone:**

    gold           px1  no baro   11 of 12 collapsed
    gold_px2       px2  no baro    1 of 1  collapsed
    gold_baro      px1  baro       1 of 1  collapsed

That interaction is why every single-factor hypothesis failed. With both applied
gold IS `flight_D_best`, so there is nothing to recover: use `flight_D_best`,
delete `gold_px2` / `gold_baro` / `gold_serial`, and stop calling
`ov_9281_aerial_staticinit` the gold standard in `config/README.md`.

Judge collapse on max-distance as well as MSCKF% — run `181121` passed at 42.5%
MSCKF while sitting 1225 m off.

**`vio_kill.sh` killed the entire desktop session at 18:02:30.** It reaps a
zombie by killing the parent; `systemd --user` is a SUBREAPER for the login
session, so orphans reparent to it rather than to pid 1, and the "not pid 1"
guard did not catch it. Now fixed with an allow-list — only recognised
launchers can be killed. If you write anything similar, allow-list the parent.

Ctrl+Z on a replay leaves a STOPPED launcher that never reaps its children;
the zombies block every later run and cannot be killed. Use Ctrl+C.
